# **Initiative definition**

This initiative is a public framework and pattern library for designing enterprise AI systems beyond chat. That definition should remain stable because it is clear, memorable, and broad enough to hold the full ambition of the work without losing precision.

The term framework signals that the initiative helps teams reason about enterprise AI as a system. The term pattern library signals that it is also practical: it provides reusable structures, interaction models, and reference patterns that teams can apply to real product work.

The phrase beyond chat is central to the positioning. It makes clear that the initiative is not limited to conversational interfaces, assistant panels, or chat-first product logic. It addresses the broader design of enterprise intelligence across workflows, surfaces, modalities, review states, permissions, and levels of autonomy.

# **Mission**

The mission of this initiative is to create a shared language, reference model, and reusable design library for building enterprise AI systems across workflows, modalities, surfaces, and levels of autonomy.

This mission matters because enterprise teams often lack a common vocabulary for discussing how intelligence should behave inside products. Without that shared language, design, product, and engineering conversations remain fragmented. Teams talk about features, models, and interfaces, but not about confidence, review, escalation, orchestration, reversibility, or the conditions under which AI should recommend, guide, or act.

The mission is therefore both conceptual and operational. It is about giving teams a stronger way to think and a more usable way to build.

# **Vision**

The vision is to become the category-defining public reference for how enterprise AI products should be designed—across workflows, intelligence layers, modalities, trust models, and future interaction environments.

This vision should remain ambitious. The initiative should not position itself as a pattern blog, a gallery, or a narrow design resource. It should aim to become the reference people use when they need to understand how enterprise AI products should be structured today and how they should evolve as interaction models expand.

The inclusion of future interaction environments is deliberate. It keeps the initiative from being trapped inside the conventions of current software alone and leaves room for multimodal, ambient, spatial, and post-screen expressions of enterprise intelligence.

# **Public promise**

The public promise of the initiative is simple but expansive: a new design language for enterprise AI—spanning patterns, modalities, surfaces, trust, and orchestration.

This wording works because it captures both breadth and intent. It does not present the initiative as a repository of examples. It presents it as a design language: something foundational enough to shape how enterprise AI is understood, discussed, and designed.

# **Why now**

The timing for this initiative is strong because most public guidance on AI product design is still incomplete for enterprise reality. Much of the visible discourse remains centered on chat interfaces, consumer interaction models, or short-term feature tactics. The result is a landscape rich in examples but thin in structure.

Enterprise conditions are different. Teams must account for workflow complexity, role-based permissions, trust boundaries, reviewability, reversibility, governance, latency, auditability, escalation paths, and implementation constraints. These conditions shape the design of AI products directly, yet public references rarely treat them as first-order design concerns.

At the same time, enterprise AI is already moving beyond assistant-style behavior. Intelligence is beginning to operate as retrieval, explanation, recommendation, generation, monitoring, coordination, and action across products. Teams need a stronger public foundation for designing systems when AI is no longer confined to a single panel, a single response type, or a single turn of interaction. That is the gap this initiative is positioned to fill.

# **Core point of view**

The central point of view behind this initiative is that enterprise AI is not a chatbot feature. It is a distributed layer of intelligence that operates across products, workflows, systems, permissions, memory, review states, automation, and surrounding context.

That distinction matters because many teams still frame AI as an assistant inserted into an existing product. In enterprise settings, that framing is already too small. Intelligence often needs to operate across multiple moments of work, multiple surfaces, multiple roles, and multiple levels of control. It must support not only answers, but progress; not only generation, but decision support; not only interaction, but orchestration.

The initiative should therefore advance a broader model of enterprise intelligence: one in which AI is expressed across the system rather than isolated in a box.

# **Beyond one interface pattern**

One of the clearest implications of this point of view is that enterprise AI cannot be reduced to a single interface pattern. Chat matters, but it is only one pattern among many.

Some enterprise tasks are well suited to conversational flows. Others require guided steps, inline assistance, structured review, proactive signaling, rich visual summaries, side-panel orchestration, low-friction approvals, or ambient interventions. In many cases, the strongest experience combines several of these rather than relying on one alone.

This is a critical principle for the initiative. The design process should begin with the nature of the work, the decision, the level of risk, and the workflow context—not with a default assumption that every AI capability should be wrapped in chat.

# **Differentiation**

This initiative stands apart because it addresses a gap that most existing AI design guidance does not cover well. Its differentiation is structural, not cosmetic.

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| **1. Beyond chat by design** | Chat is included, but intentionally decentered in favor of a broader set of enterprise interaction patterns. |
| **2. Enterprise-first** | The framework takes workflow complexity, role-based access, governance, review, approval, escalation, and implementation constraints seriously. |
| **3. Multimodal and future-facing** | It does not assume the future of enterprise AI will be text-only or screen-only and explicitly makes room for multimodal, ambient, and emerging spatial experiences. |
| **4. Trust as product architecture** | Trust is designed directly into the interaction model through permissioning, visibility, reversibility, auditability, confidence handling, and human control. |
| **5. Framework and library together** | The initiative offers both a strategic model and reusable building blocks so teams can think clearly and design concretely. |
| **6. System-aware, not screen-bound** | It helps teams think about how intelligence is delivered across moments, surfaces, environments, and autonomy levels—not just inside a single screen. |

# **Who this is for**

This initiative should remain design-led, but it should not be design-only. Its usefulness depends on whether it can support the cross-functional teams that are actually shaping enterprise AI products.

The audience therefore spans three levels:

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| **Primary audience** | Product designers, AI and UX designers, design technologists, product managers, and product strategy or innovation leads. These are the people most likely to use the framework to shape product direction, interaction models, and experience strategy. |
| **Secondary audience** | Engineering leads, front-end engineers, AI product builders, design systems teams, and researchers. They need a framework that translates high-level AI thinking into patterns, structures, and implementation-aware models. |
| **Tertiary audience** | Business stakeholders, transformation leaders, heads of product and design, and founders building enterprise AI platforms. For them, the initiative should articulate what enterprise AI design maturity looks like and why it matters. |

# **What it helps people do**

This initiative should help teams make better decisions about enterprise AI products. It should help them identify which AI pattern fits a specific enterprise problem, whether an experience should be conversational, inline, guided, proactive, multimodal, or agentic, and which modality best suits the work being done.

It should also help teams determine the right level of autonomy for the task, the right trust mechanisms for the context, and the right conditions for recommendation, review, handoff, and action.

Most importantly, it should help teams think beyond outputs. The deeper design challenge in enterprise AI is not only what the system says or generates. It is how the system behaves inside real work: how it appears, when it intervenes, what it explains, what it remembers, how it coordinates, what it can act on, and how users remain in control.

|  |
| --- |
| **Decision support questions**  **•** What is the right AI pattern for this enterprise problem?  **•** Should this experience be conversational, inline, guided, proactive, ambient, multimodal, or agentic?  **•** When should the system recommend, and when should it act?  **•** How should trust, permission, confidence, review, and reversibility shape the experience?  **•** How should the product behave at low, medium, and high autonomy? |

# **Scope and non-goals**

The scope of this initiative should be broad enough to define a serious public reference, but bounded enough to preserve coherence.

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| **In scope**  **•** Enterprise AI design principles  **•** A public taxonomy for enterprise AI interaction  **•** Pattern libraries for AI behaviors and experiences  **•** Modality and surface frameworks  **•** Trust, governance, autonomy, and control models  **•** Workflow recipes, annotated examples, and future-facing interaction models grounded in real product logic | **Non-goals**  **•** Vendor-specific product documentation  **•** Model benchmarking or general AI news  **•** Pure prompt-engineering content  **•** Deep machine learning architecture material  **•** Generic UI components without AI behavior  **•** Speculative concept art without operating logic |

# **Success criteria**

This initiative succeeds if people immediately understand that it is larger than a pattern gallery and more durable than a trend artifact. It should provide a stronger vocabulary for enterprise AI and become useful across design, product, and engineering conversations.

It should also become referenceable in broader discussions about AI UX, copilots, agentic systems, automation, and enterprise product design. Its long-term value will depend on whether it feels coherent, extensible, and grounded in a recognizable point of view.

|  |
| --- |
| **The initiative is successful when it:**  **•** Is immediately recognized as more than a pattern gallery  **•** Improves how cross-functional teams discuss enterprise AI  **•** Gives the field a stronger and more durable vocabulary  **•** Provides a public reference that others cite and build from  **•** Establishes a distinct, credible, future-facing point of view |
