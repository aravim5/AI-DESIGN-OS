## What This Example Shows

This example illustrates how to design an AI system for a high-stakes, regulated review workflow — where every recommendation must be traceable, every action must be authorized, and the system must know when it is out of its depth.

The scenario: a compliance analyst uses an AI assistant to review a set of documents for regulatory obligations before they are submitted externally.

## What the System Does

1. **Document ingestion** — analyst uploads or links documents for review
2. **Issue detection** — system identifies potential issues, gaps, or required disclosures
3. **Evidence-backed findings** — each finding shows the specific passage, the relevant regulation, and the system's reasoning
4. **Low-confidence flagging** — where the system is uncertain, it says so explicitly and asks for human judgment
5. **Approval checkpoint** — analyst must explicitly approve or reject each flagged finding before any submission proceeds
6. **Audit trail** — full record of findings shown, human decisions made, and final submission state

## Design Decisions

**Why Evidence-Backed Answer for each finding, not just a summary?**
Compliance work requires traceable rationale. An analyst who cannot see what triggered a finding cannot take professional responsibility for approving or rejecting it.

**Why Low-Confidence Recovery?**
Regulations are complex and context-sensitive. The system will encounter cases where it genuinely cannot determine compliance status. Those cases must be escalated to human judgment — not resolved by the system through a confident-sounding but uncertain answer.

**Why Approval Checkpoint, not auto-flag-and-proceed?**
The analyst is professionally and legally accountable for the submission. The system cannot remove that accountability by acting autonomously. The checkpoint ensures accountability stays with the human.

## Trust Requirements

- Every finding must cite the specific document passage and the applicable rule
- The system's confidence level must be disclosed — not normalized to appear high
- The analyst must be able to challenge a finding and provide a counter-rationale
- The audit trail must capture: finding generated, analyst review, decision made, rationale provided, submission state
- The system must not submit documents — only the analyst can commit to submission

## Common Failure Modes

- **Confident-sounding but wrong findings** — analyst approves without real scrutiny; error propagates to submission
- **Missing low-confidence disclosure** — system does not surface what it is unsure about; analyst assumes completeness
- **Audit gap** — logging captures submission but not the AI's contribution and the analyst's review decisions
- **Compliance theater** — approval checkpoint is a single click with no real review; accountability is formal but not substantive

## What Success Looks Like

- Analyst can review a complex document set faster than manual review alone
- Every finding is traceable to source text and applicable rule
- Uncertain cases are clearly flagged and escalated, not silently resolved
- Audit trail supports supervisor review, regulatory inquiry, or error investigation
- System gets better over time as analyst feedback is captured
